03 Practice

Core focus:
constitutional & administrative law.

The part of the law that governs what the State may do to you — and what you can do about it. Most people never learn they have a remedy. Here it is, in one picture.

The right

  • Art. 14Equality before the law
  • Art. 19Freedoms — speech, trade, movement
  • Art. 21Life & personal liberty

The remedy

Writ petition A direct challenge to State action — heard on affidavit, not on trial.

The forum

  • Art. 226High Court of Kerala — fundamental and other legal rights
  • Art. 32Supreme Court — fundamental rights only

The five writs

  • Habeas CorpusProduce the person — against unlawful detention
  • MandamusDo your duty — compels a public authority to act
  • CertiorariQuash it — sets aside an illegal order
  • ProhibitionStop — halts a body exceeding its jurisdiction
  • Quo WarrantoBy what authority — challenges a public appointment

04 What happens next

Three steps. Two of them have a date on them.

Most legal websites promise speed. Only part of this is inside anyone’s control, so here is exactly which part.

  1. 01Same day

    Contact, and understanding the issue

    You send a message describing what happened. Questions come back only where they’re needed to see the shape of the problem — not a document request list.

    You get: a straight answer on whether there is a legal question at all.

  2. 02Within three days

    A 1:1 meeting, and an action plan

    We meet — in person at the Kochi office, or by video. You leave with a written plan: what is filed, where, in what order, and what it will cost.

    You get: a plan on paper, and a fixed point of contact.

  3. 03Cannot be dated

    Mediation, or court

    Some matters settle across a table. Others are argued. Which road we take is decided together — but once it is in a court’s hands, the calendar belongs to the court.

    You get: honest reporting at every stage, and no invented timeline.
